How it works

From a phone on the roadside to a vet's table

First, report

Photo, place, severity

Upload a photo of the animal and rate the injury from 1 to 5. If the animal moves, upload the same photo with the new spot and its case is updated.

Then, triage

An urgency score

Every case gets a score, so the coordinator always sees the most urgent animal at the top of the queue.

Finally, dispatch

The nearest free van

The coordinator deploys a van from the closest centre that has one free. When the rescue is done, the van returns to the fleet.

About

"Bezubaan" means the one without a voice.

Strays on campus get hurt by vehicles, fights and illness. Someone usually notices. BEZUBAAN Saathi gives that person one place to report, and gives the rescue team a queue that puts the most urgent animal first.

What the system does

  • One animal, one report: each new photo is compared with every waiting case in three layers: photo fingerprint (pHash), wound and marking patterns (OpenCV ORB), and an AI model (CLIP) that recognises the same animal from a different angle, pose or background. A match updates the same case instead of creating a duplicate.
  • Triage: every pending case is scored and the queue is sorted by that score.
  • Dispatch and resolve: the nearest centre with a free van is assigned. When the rescue is done, the case is resolved and the van returns to the fleet.
  • Each case keeps one reference photo so later photos can be compared with it.
